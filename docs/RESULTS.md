## Overview

This document summarizes the empirical results of training multiple 1D-CNN architectures for BISINDO (Bahasa Isyarat Indonesia) static letter recognition. All models share the same dataset (`landmarks_captured_v2.csv`, 138,471 samples, 26 letters A–Z), the same input features (63 hand-landmark coordinates, or 84 for the 2-hand variant), and the same train/test split (85/15 stratified, `random_state=42`).

**Scope**: CNN-only. Sklearn baselines (Random Forest, SVM) are out of scope for this submission and are not included in this comparison.

## Champion Selection Rationale

`cnn_balanced` is the production-grade choice for three reasons:

1. **Highest accuracy (0.9912)** — beats the next-best by 0.3 percentage points.
2. **Highest F1 (0.9912)** — consistent with accuracy, indicating balanced precision and recall (no class collapse).
3. **Tuned for class balance** — its training data was rebalanced to ~5000 samples per letter (vs the natural 5015–6388 range in `landmarks_captured_v2.csv`), which reduces variance across under-represented letters.

The 5× slower training (3704 s vs 894 s for `cnn_final`) is acceptable because training is one-shot.

## Limitations

See `docs/LIMITATIONS.md` for the full discussion. Key points:

- Only 2 of 8 models (`cnn`, `cnn_2hand`) could be independently re-evaluated from checkpoint because the others (`cnn_balanced` and friends) have architectures not directly reconstructable from `state_dict` alone — the model class used at training time is not preserved in this repo. Their numbers come from the `*_metrics.json` files at training time.
- No k-fold cross-validation. Single train/test split reported.
- Confusion matrix and per-class breakdowns are not reported for metrics-only models — they would require successful re-evaluation.
